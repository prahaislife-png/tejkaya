The House · Origin

Radiance, embodied.

तेज काया

Tej Kaya exists because Ayurveda has been loved in Indian homes for millennia — and too often presented, in commerce, as something one would rather hide.

A contemporary Indian house

Not a revival. A standard.

The name is a pairing: तेज, the gleam of well-being; काया, the body that carries it. Together they describe a simple ambition — that classical care should feel as inevitable on a marble vanity as a bottle of perfume.

We look toward the sophistication of India’s finest botanical houses, then step aside to build our own grammar: forest and terracotta, cream ceramic, gold used as a line rather than a shout, photography that smells of kitchens and courtyards rather than clip-art herbs.

Principles

How the house decides.

I

The object must be beautiful at rest.

If it cannot live on an open shelf, it is not Tej Kaya. Packaging is not a wrapper. It is the first ritual.

II

Lineage before trend.

Formulations begin in classical categories. Contemporary botanicals may join them. The reverse — a trend in search of a Sanskrit caption — will not.

III

Language with a spine.

We will not shout medical promises to sell a jar. Copy remains editable until the laboratory and the law have spoken. Dignity is a brand asset.

IV

Room to grow the house.

Five vessels at the door. The catalogue is a living list. New products enter the same architecture: a slug, a vessel, a ritual, a garden of botanicals.

Atelier

Where the work is done.

The house is imagined from Mumbai: a city that already knows luxury and already knows home remedies, and rarely lets them meet. Tej Kaya is that meeting — brass beside linen, isabgol beside a gold-trimmed lid.

We are pre-launch. The atelier is still choosing grind, glass, and the exact weight of a morning spoon. Early access is an invitation to wait with us, not a store pretending to be open.
