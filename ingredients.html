The garden

Ingredients as provenance.

A luxury house names its materials the way a kitchen names its spices: with origin, texture, and respect. What follows is the intended palette — not a locked bill of materials.

01 · The bright fruit

Amla

Emblica officinalis · आंवला

Tannic, green-gold, the structural fruit of chyawanprash. In the house it is treated as a jewel ingredient: photographed whole, spoken of with appetite, never reduced to a vitamin call-out on a flashy banner.

In Chyawanprash

02 · The golden root

Haldi & kesar

Curcuma longa · Crocus sativus

Turmeric for earth and colour; saffron for the rare thread. Together they set the house palette — the same warmth that lives in the gold line on a label and the light in our still lifes.

03 · The rooted rasayana

Ashwagandha

Withania somnifera · अश्वगंधा

Dry, horse-scented, uncompromising. Considered for the men’s vessel and as a deeper note across the house. Inclusion, grade and quantity remain unpublished until the formula is signed.

In Prostate Care

04 · The house flower

Rose

Rosa damascena · गुलाब

Not a gourmand cloud. A cooling, discreet companion — petals in the still life, a drop in a blend, the colour of the burgundy vessel. Rose is how Tej Kaya stays Indian without becoming costume jewellery.

In Piles Care

05 · The pale husk

Isabgol

Plantago ovata · ईसबगोल

India’s most honest fibre. We refuse the plastic tub. Cream ceramic, a wooden scoop, a glass that clouds as it waits. Daily Fibre is the house’s argument that ordinary gestures can be luxurious.

In Daily Fibre
